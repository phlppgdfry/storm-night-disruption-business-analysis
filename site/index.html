IT business analysis case · Tidewell Shortsea Lines fictional operator

A storm strands 859 trailers. Who sails first — and how do you prove it before you build it?

Three sailings cancelled at 16:00. Today, half the units wait about fifteen hours for an answer, perishables spoil while frozen cargo sails, and the yard overflows. Three departments want three different fixes. This case takes the problem from that night to a tested, accepted, measurable release — and lets you replay the night yourself.

Moments that make it a case, not a template

  • Sales thought key accounts were rebooked first. The reconstruction showed they were rebooked last — their account managers start at 07:30.
  • Operations' own proposal had a flaw that only showed in simulation: ADR units drifting to Sunday while ADR slots sailed empty. Fixed by a new rule, decided by the load planner.
  • The storm drill found a severity-1 defect in my own rules — a vessel swap left reefers confirmed without plugs. Rule R-16 and an executable example came out of it.
  • The business case counts goodwill paid, not claims rejected — because weather is force majeure, and finance said so in the first meeting.

Honest limits

  • Fictional operator, people, workshops and drill. No employer or client data.
  • The simulation is a model with 44 stated assumptions; the tornado shows which ones to verify first.
  • The allocation module exists to prove the rules are precise enough to build and test — not to suggest the analyst writes production code.
  • Built by one person with AI assistance; the decisions and their reasons are in the decision log.
